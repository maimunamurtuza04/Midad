WHERE SHOULD I BEGIN?

Three books I'd recommend before any others.

These aren't necessarily the greatest philosophy books ever written. They're simply the ones I believe are the best place to begin.

The Twilight of the Idols and Antichrist

Twilight is the best primer to Nietzsche’s thought. If you want something truly mind-storming, Beyond Good and Evil is an excellent choice—but it demands an understanding of Nietzsche’s style and way of articulating his ideas. So, Twilight serves as a useful prerequisite for BGE. Twilight of the Idols is fundamentally about moving beyond traditional ways of thinking.

View on Amazon →

The Metamorphosis

The Metamorphosis is hauntingly beautiful because it captures the helplessness of becoming a burden when you can no longer contribute. What makes it worse is how deeply we’re trained to obey the system: even when something is fundamentally wrong, duty still comes first. It makes me wonder what kafka went through to actually write this piece.

View on Amazon →

Notes from Underground

Notes from Underground feels completely different. It's almost like you're stuck inside one person's head the whole time. To really appreciate it, you have to detach from your own way of thinking and try to see the world through the protagonist's mind. If you don't naturally think like him, it can just come across as repetitive, self-pitying, and honestly a bit boring.

A DIFFERENT SHELF

Books That Ask the Same Questions

No Longer Human

The book’s power lies in its visceral honesty, in how it doesn’t shy away from depicting the contradictory aspects of human experience. For many, “No Longer Human” resonates because it puts into words the inexpressible, those dark corners of the mind that are usually kept hidden. The writing may seem "unpolished" in translation, but the rawness is part of its appeal—it feels real, like an unfiltered cry from someone who is utterly lost.
